Dart Setup Guide

Get your board up properly and find darts that suit you. Pick a section below.

The measurements that matter

These are the standard competition measurements used for steel-tip darts. Use them and your home board plays exactly like the one at the pub or league venue.

WhatMeasurement
Height of the bullseye from the floor5 ft 8 in (173 cm)
Throwing distance, board face to the oche (measured along the floor)7 ft 9¼ in (237 cm)
Diagonal from the bullseye to the oche at floor level (a handy check)9 ft 7¼ in (293 cm)
Board orientation20 at the top, 3 at the bottom
Hanging the board
  1. Pick a wall that is solid and flat, with clear space either side and nothing fragile nearby.
  2. Measure 173 cm up from the floor to the centre of the bullseye and mark it.
  3. Fit the bracket so the board hangs with the 20 at the top. Many brackets let you rotate the board to even out wear. Do this regularly.
  4. Check the board is level and sits flat against the wall.
If the floor is uneven or carpeted, measure the height at the spot where you stand to throw.
Marking the oche
  1. Hold a tape measure at the board face (the front of the wires), directly below the bullseye.
  2. Measure 237 cm out along the floor in a straight line and mark it.
  3. Fix a strip of tape, a thin board or a purpose-made oche there. Stand behind it with your front foot touching but not over the line.
  4. To double check, measure diagonally from the bullseye to the oche line on the floor. It should be 293 cm.
Electronic and soft-tip boards

Soft-tip (electronic) boards use the same 173 cm height. The usual throwing distance is longer, at 8 ft (244 cm). If your board came with its own instructions, follow them.

Lighting and surroundings
  • Light the board evenly from above or from the sides. Avoid a single lamp that casts a shadow from your arm or head.
  • Avoid glare. A shiny board face under a bright spot light is hard on the eyes.
  • Protect the wall with a surround or backboard, which also catches bounce-outs.
  • Keep a clear throwing lane. Nobody should be able to walk in front of the board while you throw.
  • Pick up dropped darts straight away. A dart on the floor can break a tip or injure a foot.
Always keep children and pets well away from the throwing area and the board.
Looking after your board
  • Turn the board a little every few weeks so no one number wears out first.
  • Store sisal boards somewhere dry. Damp makes them swell and the fibres stop closing up.
  • Replace the board when it is chewed up and darts regularly fall out.

Finding darts that suit you

There is no best dart, only the one that feels right for your grip. Start with a set that is comfortable, then change one thing at a time so you know what made the difference.

Weight
  • Most steel-tip players throw somewhere between 20 g and 26 g. Starting around 22 to 24 g is common.
  • Heavier darts tend to fly flatter and feel more solid. Lighter darts can feel quicker but may need a faster, more arced throw.
  • Competition rules cap a dart at 50 g, so there is a great deal of room below that.
  • Change weight in small steps of 1 or 2 g.
The barrel
  • Material: brass is cheap and fine for beginners. Tungsten is denser, so the barrel is slimmer for the same weight. 80% tungsten is cheaper, 90% and 95% are slimmer and pricier.
  • Shape: straight barrels feel even in the hand. Torpedo barrels carry the weight toward the front. Others taper at the rear.
  • Grip: a rougher or ringed finish holds well and is good if your fingers get sweaty. A smoother one lets the dart leave your hand cleanly. Try each and see what you prefer.
The shaft (stem)
  • Shafts come in short, medium and long sizes. A longer shaft moves the flight back and can make the dart settle more quickly. A shorter shaft keeps the flight closer to the barrel.
  • Nylon shafts are cheap and flexible. Aluminium and carbon last longer.
  • If the dart wobbles in flight, trying a different length is the easiest fix.
  • Check that the shaft is screwed in snugly. A loose one will slip and move the flight.
The flights
  • Standard flights give good lift and stability and are a safe starting point.
  • Slim flights have less drag, so the dart flies a bit faster and flatter.
  • Kite and pear shapes are further options. Look for a shape that fits your throw.
  • Larger flights give more stability but more drag. Smaller flights are quicker but twitchier.
  • Replace flights as soon as they are bent or torn. They are the cheapest way to improve consistency.
Tips for trying new darts
  1. Throw a few hundred darts before judging a new set. They will feel strange at first.
  2. Change only one thing at a time: weight, then shaft length, then flights.
  3. Ask team mates if you can borrow their darts for a few throws.
  4. Pay attention to where the dart lands, not how it looks.

Where this comes from

The board height, throwing distances and the 50 g dart limit follow the World Darts Federation rules, and the soft-tip distance follows Darts Corner's guide. The advice on choosing darts is general guidance from common practice, so treat it as a starting point and trust what feels right in your hand.
